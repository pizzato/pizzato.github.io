I prefer people over computers — but most days I find myself fighting both.

Somewhere along the way I became a **Brazilian–Italian Australian** who ended up building AI systems in Sydney, raising a family, and still insisting there is time for a cold glass of wine on a Friday evening. I grew up in the south of Brazil as a proper **Gaúcho** — mate, galpão, the whole thing — and that unhurried sense of gathering around good food and good conversation has followed me across every time zone I have lived in since.

By day I work at the intersection of **computer science and data science**, mostly on language and recommender systems, occasionally wondering whether the machines we build understand anything at all. By evening I try to be a decent **father and husband**, which is harder and more rewarding than any research problem I have worked on.

When I manage to step away from a screen I play **volleyball** whenever a court is free, chase a rubber ball around a **squash** court faster than is probably sensible, and spend entirely too long thinking about my next shot in a game of **snooker** or **foosball**. I travel as often as life allows — not as a tourist ticking boxes, but slowly, looking for light and texture and the kind of place that stays with you.
